## The widening power story

The May 11 briefing led with Microsoft's $80B Azure backlog and the
128-week transformer lead time as the structural story of the cycle.
Both numbers have moved against the deployment side since then:

- Substation transformer lead times have stretched from roughly
  140 weeks in 2023, to 150 weeks in 2025, and now exceed **160 weeks
  in 2026**. Five-year backlogs on grid transformers are now standard.
- Sightline Climate tracked **12 GW** of 2026 US data center capacity
  announced across 140 projects. Only **5 GW** is actually under
  construction. **11 GW sits unbuilt** despite typical 12-18 month
  build times, because the electrical equipment is not arriving.
- Electrical equipment represents under 10% of total data center cost
  yet is **100% of the bottleneck**.
- The industry response is shifting toward on-site power generation
  to bypass high-voltage transformers entirely. **At least two major
  hyperscalers are expected to announce nuclear partnerships by year
  end.**

This is no longer a single-quarter story. The data is now telling a
consistent shape: the U.S. grid was not built for the load profile AI
training racks require (100+ kW per rack), and the gating constraint
has migrated from silicon to the electricity that runs it.
