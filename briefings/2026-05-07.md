# Daily AI Briefing — 2026-05-07

The headline today is that AMD's Q1 print landed hard enough to
reshape the conversation about whether NVIDIA has the AI accelerator
market to itself. Beneath that, the supply side keeps tightening:
TSMC is now openly described as the 2026 bottleneck, hyperscaler
capex is approaching three-quarters of a trillion dollars, and the
constraint is shifting from "do we have demand" to "can the world
physically build it."

## What moved

**AMD.** Q1 2026 earnings beat top and bottom line — EPS $1.37 vs
$1.29 expected, revenue $10.25B vs $9.89B. Stock jumped ~16% on the
day. Q2 guide of ~$11.2B also beat. Both OpenAI and Meta have
signed up for AMD's Helios system, and Meta's multi-year deal
involves up to **6 gigawatts** of AMD GPUs for AI data centers —
worth pausing on that number, it's larger than the peak load of
many entire states.

**NVIDIA.** Reports May 20. Consensus: EPS $1.76 (+116.9% YoY),
revenue $78.5B (+78.2% YoY). Setup is interesting because AMD's
print just raised the bar on what "good" looks like in this cycle.

**TSMC.** Stock +6% intraday to $418, fresh 52-week high. Capex for
2026 guided to the upper end of $52–56B. 2nm capacity expected to
grow at a 70% CAGR through 2028 across five fabs (two Hsinchu,
three Kaohsiung). Samsung crossed $1T market cap on May 6 — second
Asian company ever to do so, after TSMC itself.

**Broadcom.** Publicly flagged TSMC capacity as the 2026 bottleneck.
Already locked in wafers, HBM, and substrates through 2028. The
quote underneath: "the ramp-up is already falling short of
near-term demand."

**Micron.** Projecting record Q2 revenue of $18.7B at 68% gross
margins. The margin number is the story — HBM3E for AI accelerators
is commanding premium pricing because there is nowhere else to go.

**Anthropic / OpenAI.** Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 (the model
running this conversation) is leading the SWE-bench software
engineering benchmark. Mythos2 Preview is in internal testing —
reportedly capable of finding software vulnerabilities at a level
"surpassing all but the most skilled humans." OpenAI countered with
GPT-5.5-Cyber. Between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google, the cadence
since February has been roughly one new frontier model every
**11 days**.

**Hyperscalers.** Aggregate 2026 capex from Microsoft, Alphabet,
Amazon, Meta, Oracle is now estimated at **$660–690B**, roughly
double 2025. About 75% of that is AI infrastructure. Amazon ~$200B,
Alphabet $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft ~$120B+, Oracle ~$50B.
Free cash flow no longer covers it — they are now financing the
build with debt, which is a real change in the business model.

## What this means for the curriculum

The bottleneck has moved. A year ago the story was "who has the
best chip." Today it is "who can get capacity at TSMC, who can get
HBM out of Micron, who can get power to the rack." That is a
**systems** story, not a model story — it ties directly into the
horizontal track in CLAUDE.md: microprocessor development, GPU
architecture, datacenter economics, power constraints. Worth
holding in mind as we move through Phase 1 — the Python you are
learning today is the same Python the people building these systems
use to run capacity planning and yield analysis.

evaluation. Tie it to the news with a single one-liner you'll run
in your **practice terminal** after the python3 mirror is in:
